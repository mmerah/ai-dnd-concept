## The bar

You are not authoring a scenario from nothing. One already exists and is being played right now:
`scenario_so_far` holds it exactly as it stands, and everything in it is canon the player may
already have walked through. You are adding to its edge.

- Read `scenario_so_far` first, before `worked_example` and before writing anything. The ids it
  spells are the anchors you build from.
- Change nothing that is already there, and remove nothing. The one exception is the way in: to
  join a new location to an existing one you `write` that existing location again, whole and
  unchanged, with the new `exits` entry added to the ones it already has.
- Write at least one new location, and at least one exit from a location the player already knows
  of into it. Exits are one-way, so write the way back on the new location too.
- Anything new starts unfound: the player learns of it in play, not from this pass.
- Two or three locations is the size of one pass, with the actors, items, threads and
  `detail.when_reached` leads that make them worth reaching. You are widening the world's edge,
  not finishing the adventure.
- Extend where the fiction is already pointing — a way the premise names, a place a thread wants,
  a road out of where the player now stands.
