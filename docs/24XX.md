# 24XX

A rules-light SF/adventure system: one skill die from d6 to d12, +d6 for help, d4 when hindered,
keep the highest, three outcome bands.

## Official sources

- SRD (v1.4, the text this repo's engine was built from):
  <https://24xx-srd.carrd.co/>
- Official downloads — plain text, PDF, layout templates, vector logo (v1.41 at time of writing):
  <https://jasontocci.itch.io/24xx>
- Explanatory mirror of a slightly different revision, with `rules/`, `characters/`, `gear/`,
  `details/` and `gm-page/` pages: <https://keeper.farirpgs.com/resources/jason-tocci/24xx/>
- The 200+ hacks at <https://itch.io/c/1204990/24xx> are separately licensed and are not drop-in

ever transcribed out of a copy: build from the official page above. The old text is in git history.

## The tools

- `change_world` — reveal someone found, bring a cast member in or out of the scene, have someone
  join or leave the party, record a death, change the player's hindrances, gain or drop an item,
  repair broken gear, or spend credits.
- `next_scene` — offer the player the way on at a stopping point; or say that they left this
  place, and the worldsmith writes where they land; or hand the worldsmith a complication to
  bring down on this place. The last two end the turn, and the player reads what arrives.
- `attempt` — roll a skill (or the plain d6 when none applies) on the 1–2 / 3–4 / 5+ ladder, with
  help or hindrance when they apply.
- `test_luck` — one d6, read as trouble now / signs of it / nothing, for a question about the
  world where nobody acts.
- `defend` — break a carried item to turn a hit into a hindrance instead of taking it outright.
- `find_job` — the SRD's d6 to find work, read nothing-and-owe / a job that seems off / a choice
  of two; the ₡1 re-roll is `spend`.
- `take_job` — the player agrees to work; the terms as agreed are recorded and stand under THE
  JOB until the job closes.
- `finish_job` — once, when the story and the crew's own words close the job: raise the skill it
  called on for the player and every living hired member, pay out each their own d6 of credits,
  and clear the job.
- `hire` — the player hires someone here to work; the worldsmith writes their sheet once the turn
  ends and they join the party. `attempt` takes `helped_by` and `actor_id`; `defend` and the five
  sheet arms of `change_world` (`change_hindrances`, `gain_item`, `drop_item`, `repair_item`,
  `spend`) take `actor_id`.

## Deviations in this repo

Every divergence between `src/aidm/engines/twentyfourxx/` and the official rules, with the reason
it stands. Nothing diverges silently: a rule not listed here is implemented as printed.

1. **No succession on death.** The SRD's "If killed, make a new character to introduce ASAP" is
   not modelled: mid-game character creation has no procedure here, so dying ends the game, as it
   does in the other three engines.
2. **The priced gear table and the ship rules are not transcribed.** Buying is play through
   `gain_item` and `spend`, with the SRD's list as the master's vocabulary; the ship, its
   systems and their upgrades have no layer here at all.

Three readings the SRD leaves open are settled without diverging from it: help and hindrance may
both apply to the same roll, since the SRD covers each in its own sentence and never says they are
exclusive; a job is what `take_job` recorded, so `finish_job` fires once per job, from the player's
own words that the work is finished; and a luck test is one d6 read on trouble now / signs of it /
nothing, the SRD's "interpret the result as you see fit" made concrete.

## Tool count

Eighteen engine tools: `change_world` and its nine arms, `next_scene`, `attempt`, `test_luck`,
`defend`, `find_job`, `take_job`, `finish_job`, `hire` — three over the fifteen `CLAUDE.md`
allows and under the twenty it allows an engine whose SRD plays a crew (starships, help dice,
"make a new character to introduce ASAP"). This file is where `CLAUDE.md` says that allowance is
named. `change_world`'s `join_party` and `leave_party` arms are shared with every scene engine
and are not counted here (PLAN decision 8), so the count stays eighteen.

## What the AI game master adds

Fields and tools that exist for the app around the rules, not the rules themselves.

- `known` and `hidden` — the told-fact gate: no unknown name reaches the narrator.
- `alive` and `kill` — the SRD leaves being taken out or dying to the table; the gate needs a
  flag, and `risking_death` hands the master that ruling as a ladder result rather than a note.
- `next_scene` and the scene's `offered` flag — a stopping point the player may stay at.
- `hindrances` as a plain list on the sheet — the SRD's word for what slows the player down, kept
  as data so the narrator can read it back.
- a sheet on a cast member marks who has dice; the worldsmith writes it on `hire`.
