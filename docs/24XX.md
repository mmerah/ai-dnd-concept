## Rules

**Play:** Players describe what their characters do. The GM advises when their action is impossible,
demands a cost or extra steps, or presents a risk. Players can revise plans before committing so as
to change goal/stakes. Only roll to avoid risk.

**Rolling:** Roll a skill die — d6 by default, higher with a relevant skill, or d4 if hindered by
injury or circumstances. If helped by circumstances, roll an extra d6; if helped by an ally, they
roll their skill die and share the risk. Take the highest die.

- **1–2 Disaster.** Suffer the full risk. GM decides if you succeed at all. If risking death, you die.
- **3–4 Setback.** A lesser consequence or partial success. If risking death, you're maimed.
- **5+ Success.** The higher the roll, the better.

If success can't get you what you want (you make the shot, but it's bulletproof!), you'll at least
get useful info or set up an advantage.

**Load:** Carry as much as makes sense, but more than one bulky item may hinder you at times.

**Advancement:** After a job, each character increases a skill (none ⮕ d8 ⮕ d10 ⮕ d12) and gains
d6 credits (₡).

**Defense:** Say how one of your items breaks to turn a hit into a brief hindrance. Broken gear is
useless until repaired.

**Harm:** Injuries take time and/or medical attention to heal. If killed, make a new character to
introduce ASAP. Favor inclusion over realism.

**Principles:** Describe characters in terms of behaviors, risks, and obstacles, not skill dice.
When players roll, escalate and advance the story. Lead the group in setting lines not to cross in
play. Fast-forward, pause, or rewind/redo for pacing and safety; invite players to do likewise.
Present dilemmas you don't know how to solve. Move spotlight to give all time to shine. Test as
needed for bad luck (e.g., run out of ammo, or into guards) — roll a die to check for (1–2) trouble
or (3–4) signs of it. Improvise rulings to cover gaps in rules; on a break, revise unsatisfactory
rulings as a group.

// ADDING TO RULES: This SRD is very brief, with the hope experienced RPG players will fill in the
gaps confidently, and RPG newcomers will be free of too many preconceived notions. Anything left
vague is deliberately open to interpretation. (Like: Can you get help dice from an ally AND
circumstances on one roll? Your call!) Expand or clarify as needed. My own principles for new rules
are to minimize addition and subtraction, avoid too much bookkeeping (on top of tracking credits,
hindrances, number of bulky items, and which items are broken), and strive to use terms either
self-evident in meaning or invitingly vague.

> Mirror variants (farirpgs revision): the 3–4 result reads "If risking death, you're **injured**"

## Characters

// SRD design notes start with two slashes, like this. Other paragraphs are player/GM-facing text.

// Characters start with 6ish skill increases and/or credits' in items, possibly combining
"specialty" and "origin" (or "3 skill increases" as a stand-in).

► Choose your character's specialty.

**Face:** Skilled in Reading People (d8), Deception (d8). Take an extensive disguise wardrobe.

**Muscle:** Skilled in Intimidation (d8) and either Hand-to-hand (d8) or Shooting (d8). Take a
sword, firearm, or cyber-arm.

**Psychic:** Skilled in Telepathy (d8, sense surface thoughts), Telekinesis (d8, as strong as your
arms), or pick one at d10. Take a bottle of PsychOut (amplify powers; addictive).

**Medic:** Skilled in Medicine (d8), Electronics (d8). Take a medkit and cyber-surgery tools (bulky).

**Sneak:** Skilled in Climbing (d8), Stealth (d8). Take climbing gear and night vision goggles.

**Tech:** Skilled in Hacking (d8), Electronics (d8). Take repair tools and a custom computer (bulky).

► Choose your character's origin.

**Alien:** Invent 2 traits, like electric current, wings, natural camouflage, or six-limbed.

**Android:** You have an upgrade-ready cyber-body. Take synth skin (looks human) or a case (break
harmlessly for defense). Increase 1 skill.

**Human:** Apply 3 skill increases (from no skill ⮕ d8 ⮕ d10 ⮕ d12). You can take new skills
and/or increase skills you already have.

► Choose or invent skills (if prompted by origin).

Climbing, Connections, Deception, Electronics, Engines, Explosives, Hacking, Hand-to-hand,
Intimidation, Labor, Persuasion, Piloting, Running, Shooting, Spacewalking, Stealth, Tracking

## Gear

// If an item costs less than a new video game system, the only cost is the time it takes to get it.

► Take a comm (smartphone) and ₡2. Most items and upgrades cost ₡1 each. Ignore microcredit
transactions like a knife or a meal.

**Armor:** Vest (break once for defense), battle armor (₡2, bulky, break up to 3×), hardsuit (₡3,
bulky, break up to 3×, vacuum-rated, mag boots).

**Cybernetics:** Cyber-ear (upgrade with echolocation, vocal stress detector), cyber-eye (upgrade
with infrared, telescopic, x-ray), cyber-limb (upgrade with fast, strong, compartments, tool or
weapon implant), cranial jack (upgrade with sensory data backup, skill increase), healing nanobots,
toxin filter, voice mimic.

**Tools:** Flamethrower (bulky), low-G jetpack, med scanner, mini drone, repair tools, survey pack
(climbing gear, flare gun, tent; bulky).

**Weapons:** Grenades (4, any of fragmentation, flashbang, smoke, EMP), pistol, rifle (bulky),
shotgun (bulky), stun baton, tranq gun.

► Starships have basic versions of these functions; upgrades cost ₡10 each. In an emergency,
players pick a function to do or help with.

**Comms:** Upgrade with eavesdropper, jammer, tachyon burst (no lag in-system).

**Crafts:** Comes with escape pod. Upgrade with fighter, shuttle (reentry-rated).

**Drive:** FTL jump and sublight speeds. Upgrade with longer jumps, faster speed, greater agility.

**Equipment:** Vac suits for crew. Upgrade with armory, heavy loader, mining gear, tow cable.

**Hull armor:** Break harmlessly for defense. Upgrade with reentry-rated, sun shielding.

**Sensors:** Upgrade with deep-space, life-sign scan, planetary survey, tactical vessel scan.

**Weapons:** Deflector turrets. Upgrade with laser cutter, military-grade turret, torpedos.

## Layout notes

Not rules text; recorded from the SRD's own presentation and its itch.io release page.

- The SRD is laid out as a two-page (one-sheet) document: rules and characters on one side, gear,
  details, and setting on the other. The back page fits four d20 tables, as the design note above
  describes.
- Official releases include a plain-text SRD, B&W PDFs (singles and spreads), Affinity Publisher and
  Adobe InDesign templates, a vector logo (EPS), and Google Slides/Docs versions.
- Fonts in the Affinity template are D-DIN for headings and Roboto Condensed for body text; the
  commercial 2400 series uses different faces.
- Design stance stated on the release page: prioritize description and narrative positioning over
  detailed rules processes, take the highest die rolled over mental arithmetic, and use polyhedral
  dice over only one shape of die.

## Deviations in this repo

Every divergence between `src/aidm/engines/twentyfourxx/` and the rules above, with the reason it
stands. Nothing diverges silently: a rule not listed here is implemented as printed. Two rulings
the SRD leaves open are settled without being deviations: help is one die at most (naming both an
ally and a `helped` circumstance is refused — the SRD's own note says stacking is "your call"),
and "more than one bulky item *may* hinder you at times" is the situational adjudication the SRD
makes it — the Director names the bulky item itself as what hinders them when the load plausibly
bites. The
d20 detail tables and the d6 job-finding setup are content the SRD itself offers as "invent or
roll" and as an optional setup; scenarios here are authored ahead of play — the invent branch.

1. **The Play loop's advise-and-revise is compressed.** The SRD's GM advises when an action is
   impossible, demands a cost or extra steps, and lets players revise plans before committing;
   this app resolves one plan per player input, so refusal happens through plan validation and
   the player revises by typing again, not through table talk before the roll. A turn may now
   resolve up to three rolled beats before the player speaks again, and two guards bound that: a
   player disaster or a landed bad-luck trouble settles the turn, and the continuation prompt
   tells the Director to end the turn whenever the next move needs the player's own intent.
   Either way the turn's last beat is a settle beat: the Director is always shown what the last
   roll caused and asked what it leaves behind, but may not roll again.
2. **Harm and defence are fiction, not engine moves.** "Say how one of your items breaks to turn
   a hit into a brief hindrance" is written as a `trait-change` on the item; the engine has no
   cancel-a-hit step. A helping ally "shares the risk" the same way: the engine tracks no harm,
   so their exposure is whatever the Director writes into the beats that follow.
3. **Muscle's weapon and Android's cyber-body choice stay fiction, not gear.** The SRD asks for a
   pick — sword, firearm, or cyber-arm for Muscle; synth skin or a case for Android — the same
   way it asks every other specialty to take a kit. Every other specialty's kit lands as items the
   character carries; these two carry no `kit` in the pack, so the choice never mechanizes at
   all — it stays in the specialty's and origin's `detail` text alone.

## Engine package

How this SRD maps onto `src/aidm/engines/twentyfourxx/`:

- **Sheet** (`mechanics.py`): `specialty`, `origin`, `skills` as a d8/d10/d12 ladder with an
  unlisted skill rolling the bare d6, `credits` as a `Counter` starting at ₡2, and the `jobs`
  ledger the advancement trigger reads. The engine's own mechanics carry a `completed` tally
  game-wide — how many jobs the fiction has closed — that a newcomer's `jobs` ledger starts level
  with, so jobs done before they joined are not owed.
- **Plan** (`actions.py`): two actions — the `attempt` (`actor_id`, `goal`, the `skill` named
  from the sheet, `helped`/`hindered` as the circumstance that makes the attempt easier or
  harder, in the Director's own words, `helper_id`/`helper_skill` for an ally who helps, and
  `luck_test`) and the standalone `luck-test` (`actor_id`, `subject`) for a
  beat where nothing is attempted. The engine's own effects: `change-credits` pays or charges the
  ₡ pool for gear, repairs, debts and pay — never a roll's own outcome — and refuses an overdraw;
  `complete-job` records the fiction's own boundary — that the job is done — that advancement is
  owed against.
- **Resolver** (`actions.py`): the pool built from the skill die (or d4 when hindered) plus one
  help die — the helper's own skill die, or a flat d6 for a circumstance — keep highest, into
  the three outcome labels — `disaster`, `setback`, `success`; the bad-luck test — riding an
  attempt or standalone — rolls resolver-side into `pending_notes`.
- **Advancement** (`advance.py`): one skill step up the ladder plus d6 credits, both rolled
  resolver-side, offered once per recorded `complete-job` to every party member who hasn't yet
  taken it.
- **Creation** (`create.py`, `pack.py`, `packs/srd.json`): the specialty, origin, and skill tables
  as a content pack, with follow-up steps for a specialty's either/or training and an Alien's
  traits, typed in the player's own words with the origin's four examples offered as hints, not a
  menu; an origin's skill increases may repeat, so a Human's 3 can stack onto one skill
  (none ⮕ d8 ⮕ d10 ⮕ d12) as the SRD allows. The comm and the specialty kit land as items in the
  character's hands, a bulky one traited `bulky`; a user pack merges over the shipped one the same
  way every other engine's packs do.
- **Director** (`director.md`): teaches when an `attempt` is warranted, what the three outcomes
  mean, the load and defence rulings, and that every roll — including the bad-luck test — is the
  engine's, never its own.

Divergences live in **Deviations in this repo** above, not here.
