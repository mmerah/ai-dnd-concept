# 24XX

A rules-light SF/adventure system: one skill die from d6 to d12, +d6 for help, d4 when hindered,
keep the highest, three outcome bands.

## Official sources

- SRD (v1.4, the text this repo's engine was built from):
  <https://24xx-srd.carrd.co/>
- Official downloads — plain text, PDF, layout templates, vector logo (v1.41 at time of writing):
  <https://jasontocci.itch.io/24xx>
- Explanatory mirror of a slightly different revision, with `rules/`, `characters/`, `gear/`,
  `details/` and `gm-page/` pages: <https://keeper.farirpgs.com/resources/jason-tocci/24xx/>
- The 200+ hacks at <https://itch.io/c/1204990/24xx> are separately licensed and are not drop-in
  packs.

This file used to hold a near-verbatim extraction of the SRD. It was deleted so that no pack is
ever transcribed out of a copy: build from the official page above. The old text is in git history.

## Deviations in this repo

Every divergence between `src/aidm/engines/twentyfourxx/` and the official rules, with the reason it
stands. Nothing diverges silently: a rule not listed here is implemented as printed. Two rulings
the SRD leaves open are settled without being deviations: help is one die at most (naming both an
ally and a `helped` circumstance is refused — the SRD's own note says stacking is "your call"),
and "more than one bulky item *may* hinder you at times" is the situational adjudication the SRD
makes it — the Director names the bulky item itself as what hinders them when the load plausibly
bites. An ally who helps "shares the risk" in the fiction rather than in the engine: this SRD
tracks no harm for anyone, so every injury is a trait the Director writes, and the printed Defence
step belongs to the played character's own attempt while an ally's exposure is what the Director
writes next. The d20 detail tables and the d6 job-finding setup are content the SRD itself offers
as "invent or roll" and as an optional setup; scenarios here are authored ahead of play — the
invent branch.

1. **A killed player character is replaced by a companion, not by a new character.** The SRD says
   "If killed, make a new character to introduce ASAP. Favor inclusion over realism." A death that
   leaves companions ends the turn on a succession decision — one option per companion travelling
   with the player — and taking one moves `Game.player_id` to them, so play carries on from the
   next turn. Mid-game character creation is not modelled: the replacement is someone already in
   the fiction. Dying alone still ends the game, which is the SRD's own "ASAP" read at its
   narrowest. The death of an NPC, a companion included, is implemented as printed.
